# Hybrid search (BM25 + vectors) vs the Step 5.4 baseline

> Status: model-free run. The nightly build sandbox cannot download sentence-transformers models, so the vector side here is the `hashing-512` bag-of-words embedder from Step 5.4, not MiniLM. To get the real comparison, run locally (needs `pip install -e ".[rag]"`): `notetaker eval-retrieval --embedder hashing-512 --embedder sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 --max-words 120 --retriever vector bm25 hybrid hybrid-linear -o reports/hybrid.md`, which overwrites this file.

## Findings so far

- **BM25 is the best single retriever on this set.** At 120-word passages it lifts recall@5 from 0.837 (the 5.4 baseline) to 0.913 and MRR from 0.717 to 0.782. The gain is almost all on paraphrase questions (recall@5 0.692 to 0.846): BM25's IDF weighting lets the one or two rare words a paraphrase keeps carry the match, where hashing gives every word similar weight.
- **Fusing two keyword methods does not help top-5.** RRF of hashing + BM25 gets the best recall@10 (0.962) but lands between the two on recall@5 (0.875). That is expected: fusion pays off when the retrievers make *different* mistakes, and hashing and BM25 are both word matchers, so hashing mostly adds noise. The real test of hybrid search is MiniLM + BM25 (command above).
- **Linear fusion is sensitive to alpha.** A sweep at 120 words gave recall@5 = 0.913 at alpha 0.2 and 0.3, 0.837 at 0.5 and 0.798 at 0.7 (alpha is the weight on the vector score). It only matches BM25 when it mostly *is* BM25. RRF needs no such tuning, which is why it is the default `hybrid`.
- **60-word passages are worse for every retriever**, confirming the 5.4 result.
- One question is worth about 0.04 here (26 questions), so treat smaller gaps as ties.

---

Question set: `eval/retrieval/questions.json` (26 questions, 4 lectures). Index: 20 passages. Generated by `notetaker eval-retrieval`.

recall@k: share of answer spans found in the top k passages. MRR: mean of 1 / rank of the first relevant passage (0 if not in the top 10). Chance: expected score of a random ordering. With 26 questions, one question is worth about 0.04, so smaller gaps are ties.
